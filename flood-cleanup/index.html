A sump pump that quits, a sewer that backs up, a pipe that lets go, rising river water, storm surge or a hard rain can each flood a property, and the damage should be handled by professionals without delay. Floodwater is almost always rated Category 2 (gray water) or Category 3 (black water), so it carries bacteria, contaminants and possibly hazardous material that can make people seriously ill. Davis Restoration handles emergency flood cleanup across Davis and answers 24/7 to pump out the water, sanitize what it touched and restore your property safely.

Cleanup opens with rapid extraction: truck-mounted units and submersible pumps that pull thousands of gallons an hour out of the building. After that, IICRC-certified technicians on our team measure how contaminated the area is, then sanitize it with antimicrobial products, HEPA-filtered air cleaning and targeted disinfection of all surfaces the water reached. Materials beyond saving, like contaminated drywall, damaged insulation and saturated carpet pad, are removed and disposed of the right way.

Then the drying begins: high-velocity air movers and commercial dehumidifiers of the LGR (low-grain refrigerant) type go in to pull moisture out of the building's structural parts. We check moisture levels every day with calibrated meters and thermal imaging so the drying is complete and mold does not get a start. Every stage of the job is recorded for your claim: photographs, detailed moisture maps, logs of the equipment used and the scope-of-work reports that insurance adjusters require.

Flood Cleanup Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Seal foundation cracks and apply a waterproofing coating to basement walls
  • A backwater valve on your sewer line keeps a backup from pushing sewage indoors
  • Being outside a designated flood zone does not rule out flooding, so think about flood coverage
  • Debris in a gutter or storm drain blocks proper flow, so clean them out regularly
  • In areas of the property that tend to flood, raise electrical systems and appliances off the floor
  • Make sure the ground slopes away from your foundation on every side

Why Professional Flood Cleanup Matters

Getting the standing water out is only one part of cleaning up after a flood. Floodwater carries bacteria, sewage, chemicals and other contaminants that are serious health hazards. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Improper cleanup also tends to miss moisture you cannot see, and within days that trapped water can cause a major mold problem. Our technicians rely on psychrometric calculations, meter readings and thermal imaging to verify that each affected spot, from the framing to the subfloor to the inside of the walls, meets accepted drying standards. Nothing else reliably heads off secondary damage, and that damage can run thousands more to remediate, so we dry by the numbers.

How to Tell You Need Flood Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Cracked foundation walls, low doorways or window wells letting water inside
  • Basement flooding after the sump pump quit in a downpour
  • Stains along baseboards and lower walls that mark the flood level
  • Mud, silt or debris left on floors once the water goes down
  • An overflowing washing machine, dishwasher or water heater
  • Soft, wet drywall or carpeting that is soaked through
  • Water collecting along the foundation because the yard is saturated

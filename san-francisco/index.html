Common Water Damage Issues in San Francisco

Water damage does not always arrive as a sudden gush across the kitchen. Below are the situations our crews are called to most often around San Francisco, and what each one tends to involve.

Hidden leaks: A pinhole in a supply line or a loose drain fitting can drip inside a wall for months. Watch for a water bill that climbs for no reason, soft spots on the floor, and paint that blisters. Our technicians trace the wet area with moisture meters and thermal cameras, without tearing out more wall than needed. Even a slow leak matters, because wood and drywall that stay damp will rot and grow mold.

Sewage: When a main drain line clogs, wastewater comes back up through floor drains, tubs and toilets on the lowest level. Tree roots, grease buildup and heavy rain overloading the public system are the common triggers. Sewage counts as Category 3, the most contaminated class, and it is not safe to clean up bare-handed. Carpet, padding and other absorbent materials that got wet usually cannot be saved.

Storms: Hard rain can outrun gutters and yard drains, then pool against the house. It enters at door thresholds, low windows and the garage, wherever the ground slopes toward the building. Floodwater from outside picks up soil, fertilizer and whatever else it crossed, so we treat it as contaminated. A big enough storm can cause it at any time of year, and San Francisco properties are no exception.

Crawlspace: In homes built over a crawl space, few people look underneath, so moisture there builds up unseen. Standing water, a torn vapor barrier or a leaking pipe overhead keeps the air below your floors humid. You may notice cupped floorboards, a stale smell in the living area or insulation hanging loose. Our team removes the water, dries the framing and can point out what is letting moisture in.

Condensate: An air conditioner pulls gallons of water out of the air on a humid day. That water should leave through a small drain line, which clogs easily with algae and dust. A blockage makes the drip pan spill over, wetting the ceiling or floor near the indoor unit. In San Francisco we often find this only after a stain or sagging drywall gives it away, so a yearly drain check helps.

Simple Ways to Keep a San Francisco Home Dry

A flood in the house feels like it came from nowhere, yet most trace back to wear that built up over months or years. For homeowners in San Francisco, prevention is mainly a matter of knowing where to look.

Pipes and fittings top the list. A water bill that climbs for no reason, or pressure that seems too high, can signal a concealed leak or strain on the system. Appliances are second. Run the dishwasher and the washer only while someone is in the house, and change their hoses before they show cracks. Third is the outside of the building. Rain gets in through damaged roofing, failed flashing, clogged gutters and ground that tilts toward the walls.

Leaving the house empty for a while? Turn the water off at the main, or ask someone to check the house. Davis Restoration is glad to talk through prevention with any homeowner in San Francisco. And if the worst happens anyway, a fast call keeps a small leak from turning into a large repair.

What to Do Before Our San Francisco Crew Arrives

What you should do before we arrive depends on what kind of water you are looking at. Clean water from a supply pipe or a tap left running is the least risky. It is fine to step through in footwear, towel it up and carry your things clear. Water from a washing machine, dishwasher or aquarium is dirtier, so wear gloves and wash your hands afterward. Anything from a sewer, or water that came in from outside, is hazardous, so shut the room and stay away from it.

Whatever the type, a few things help our San Francisco crew start faster. Clear a path from the door to the wet area and move cars so the truck can park close. Note the time you discovered the problem and your best guess at the source. Find your insurance policy number if it is easy to get to.

Time matters because clean water does not stay clean. Left sitting in carpet and drywall, it picks up contamination and can support mold within 24 to 48 hours. That is why the opening hour counts, even when a San Francisco leak seems small.
